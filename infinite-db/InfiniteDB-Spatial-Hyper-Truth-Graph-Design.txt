1. Purpose and Scope
This document records a deliberate change in what InfiniteDB is for. The system was conceived as an embedded spatial database: a store of entities, relationships, and signals addressed by physical integer coordinates and indexed along a Hilbert curve. The design theory developed across recent architecture sessions reframes the system as the storage substrate for a spatial hyper-truth graph: a durable, geometrically indexed record of assertions made by independent parties, over which truth is computed rather than stored.
The scope boundary is strict and is restated throughout this document because it disciplines every design decision. InfiniteDB is the storage mechanism only. It stores testimony and its derived geometry, and it makes both efficiently queryable. It does not own the rules of any domain. Arbiters, engineering codes, plausibility envelopes, conflict resolution semantics, and human judgment all live above the database, in applications. The database supplies four things to those layers: durable parallel ingestion of assertions, structural directionality of hyperedges, fast geometric and topological queries, and a frame mechanism through which any party can ask for its view of the truth.
The document is organized in four movements: where the system stands today (Section 2), the theoretical shift that motivates the refactor (Section 3), the target model and the storage architecture that realizes it (Sections 4 and 5), and a staged refactor plan with honest costs (Sections 6 and 7).
2. Where InfiniteDB Stands Today
2.1 Architectural pillars
InfiniteDB is an embedded database implemented in Rust. Its identity rests on a small set of committed primitives. Spaces are named datasets of fixed dimensionality; an address is a space plus an integer coordinate point. All keys are ordered along a Hilbert curve computed with Skilling's 2004 algorithm, so spatial proximity becomes key proximity and range queries decompose into Hilbert interval scans. Writes are append-only through a write-ahead log and accumulate in live tails before being sealed into immutable blocks; the sealed block store, not the WAL, is the source of truth, with the WAL treated as transient scaffolding whose deletion is gated on merge, replication confirmation, and collision evaluation. Reads are multi-versioned: every record carries a revision, snapshots assemble immutable read views from sealed blocks, and tombstones suppress prior live records without erasing them. Branches provide parallel lines of development with overlay reads and merge strategies. The system is offline-first, with sync and replication built around per-operation outbox state.
2.2 The hyperedge model as built
Relationships are N-ary hyperedges: an identifier, a user-defined kind string, a list of endpoint references, optional weight, metadata, and a validity window expressed in revisions. Each endpoint carries a semantic role label, a space, and a coordinate node. Structurally the model is undirected: roles and kinds provide only application-layer directional convention, which the storage and index layers cannot see or enforce. A reverse endpoint index in a reserved index space maps each endpoint to its incident hyperedge identifiers through a coordinate layout that places space and endpoint coordinates as a queryable prefix ahead of the edge identifier dimensions, so incidence lookup is a prefix subscope scan rather than a full edge-space scan. Traversal is a bounded breadth-first walk that expands symmetrically: from any reached node, every other endpoint of every incident active edge is enqueued, filtered only by kind and validity. An optional centroid keying mode addresses an edge at the centroid of its endpoints so that spatially related edges cluster on the curve.
2.3 Concurrency posture
The near-term concurrency decision is a coarse mutex around the database to unblock multi-threaded callers, with interior mutability treated as a deliberate future milestone with its own test suite. The designed-but-not-yet-landed concurrent WAL architecture is directly relevant to this document: per-session WAL files give each writer an independent durable stream; revision identifiers are Hybrid Logical Clock values packing timestamp, session, and sequence into a single 128-bit ordering key; Intent Checkpoints separate durability from commit intent and mark logical operation boundaries; and a timed fast path attempts direct seal before falling back to the WAL. Collision detection is performed at Intent Checkpoint boundaries, with semantic resolution explicitly deferred to the application layer.
2.4 Known gaps entering this refactor
Four gaps identified in prior sessions are absorbed into this design rather than tracked separately: structural directed hyperedge support; a staleness query capability keyed on source revision; index-backed rather than scan-assisted endpoint queries in all paths; and traversal that surfaces wave-front level assignments. Each turns out to be a fragment of the larger model described below: directionality is the structural prerequisite, staleness propagation is a directional closure, and level assignments are the topological strata that only directed traversal can produce meaningfully.
3. The Theoretical Shift: From Stored State to Stored Testimony
3.1 Two kinds of truth
Classical databases conflate two different things at the commit point. The first is truth of occurrence: the fact that a particular party asserted a particular claim at a particular time. This kind of truth is intrinsically parallel; one engineer's assertion requires no coordination with another's to be true, and it remains true forever even if the claim it carries is later judged wrong. The second is truth of state: the proposition that the claim correctly describes the world or the model. This is not a fact but a judgment, a function applied to the body of assertions. The conventional commit makes a write simultaneously durable and globally visible as current state, and it is the second half of that bargain, instant global judgment, that forces synchronization. Durability alone never required it.
InfiniteDB drops the requirement that judgment happen at write time. The only synchronous obligation in the system is each stream's durability to itself: an assertion must survive the crash of the machine that made it, which is an append to that session's own WAL, coordinating with no one. Everything above occurrence, including the current state of the model itself, is derived, plural, and computed in parallel.
3.2 Assertions, arbiters, judgments, and frames
An assertion is the unit of storage: a claim (a node, a directed hyperedge, a signal sample) made by an identified stream at an HLC-stamped moment, optionally carrying the provenance of the frame its author was reading when the claim was made. An arbiter is any process, deterministic or human, that evaluates assertions and emits judgments: claims about claims, stored as ordinary assertions in the arbiter's own stream, with their own provenance and revisions. A frame is the answer to the question every query must now declare: which assertion streams am I reading, through which arbiters' judgments, as of when. Branches are revealed as named frames; the main branch is demoted from the truth to one privileged arbiter's output stream. Conflict ceases to be an error condition: contradictory assertions coexist as occurrence-truths, adjudicated by arbiters when and where a single answer is required.
3.3 Truth as convergence
The definition of truth this architecture implements is the pragmatist one: truth is not a stored value but the limit toward which assertion streams converge under sustained error correction against reality-anchored frames. The database cannot contain truth even in principle; what it can do, and what no conventional store can, is contain the entire convergence process. A misconception, the judgment that caught it, the correction that superseded it, and the authoring frame that explains it are all permanent, spatially indexed records. The trajectory is the asset: it powers convergence measurement, anticipatory warning, rule refinement, and knowledge transfer, all of which are application capabilities built on storage primitives this document specifies.
3.4 The boundary restated
The rule lives outside the database; the cheapness lives inside it. The database does not know what a floor is, that load flows downward, or what tolerance a code permits. It knows direction, geometry, incidence, revision, stream, and frame, and it makes questions phrased in those terms answerable in microseconds. The engineering application owns rules and their interpretation; the database owns making arbitration affordable and its outputs durable. The one place synchronization is legitimately required sits entirely outside the database: irreversible action. The architectural law is to synchronize at actuation boundaries, never at assertion boundaries.
4. The Target Model: Spatial Hyper-Truth Graph
4.1 Assertions and random arrival
Data arrives in the order people produce it, which is to say effectively at random: out of sequence, concurrently, from sessions that were offline for hours or weeks, in bulk imports interleaved with interactive edits. The target model treats this as the normal case rather than a disturbance. Because assertions are append-only facts ordered by Hybrid Logical Clock values rather than by arrival, ingestion order is irrelevant to correctness: the partial order across streams is reconstructed from the HLC stamps, not from the sequence in which records reached disk. Because keys are spatial rather than temporal, Hilbert locality is also independent of arrival order; an edge asserted today about a region modeled last year lands beside its neighbors on the curve regardless. Identity allocation is per-stream (session-prefixed identifiers), so no writer ever coordinates with another to create. The write path contracts to its minimum: validate locally, stamp, append to the session's own stream.
4.2 Directed hyperedges as the structural primitive
A directed hyperedge is a partition of its endpoint set into a tail set and a head set: the relationship flows from everything in the tail to everything in the head. This is strictly more expressive than a directed graph edge, because a single hyperedge with several tails and one head expresses conjunctive dependency natively: these inputs jointly produce this output; this slab stands only if all of these columns are present. Each endpoint therefore gains a structural polarity (tail, head, or neutral) that is orthogonal to its semantic role label. Roles remain application vocabulary; polarity becomes a topological invariant the storage and index layers understand and enforce. The edge itself carries a directionality mode, and validation enforces the matching invariant: a directed edge requires at least one tail and at least one head, while undirected edges keep the existing rules. The kind catalog is extended so a kind can be registered as obligately directed, obligately undirected, or free, allowing adapters to be rejected at the door when they assert a bears-on relationship without a valid partition.
Directionality is the prerequisite for the model's most important capabilities. Asymmetric closures (what does this column carry; where does this load go) become one-sided index scans rather than symmetric fan-out with application-side role filtering. Per-kind acyclicity becomes checkable, making topological ordering and staged recomputation possible. Staleness propagation, the missing source-revision capability, becomes coherent: what is downstream of a changed node is a forward closure, and whether a derived value is stale is a backward check over input revisions. And in a physically grounded coordinate space, every directed edge acquires a derived flow vector, the head centroid minus the tail centroid, turning direction into literal geometry (Section 5.4).
4.3 The query of truth
The frame is the query-of-truth mechanism, and its efficiency is a first-class requirement of this refactor, equal in rank to write throughput. A frame query names three coordinates of an epistemic position. First, the assertion scope: which streams, branches, or stream classes are admitted as testimony; the raw union of everything is a valid scope, as is a single engineer's stream. Second, the judgment overlay: which arbiters' judgment streams are consulted, and with what effect (filter out condemned assertions, annotate them, or surface only the contested ones). Third, the revision ceiling: the familiar as-of, now understood as one axis of a three-axis frame. Supersession is resolved inside the frame: a claim is current within a frame if no admitted later assertion supersedes it and no consulted judgment closes it. Section 5.5 specifies how this resolution is made index-resident rather than computed by post-hoc joins.
Three frame queries illustrate the range. A construction-facing frame reads the buyer's selection judgments over the code arbiter's pass judgments over all design streams as of contract date: the buildable truth. A review-facing frame reads the raw union of two engineers' streams with the validation arbiter's conflict judgments overlaid: show me everywhere in the building where these two currently disagree, as a Hilbert range scan with spatial results. A retrospective frame reads one engineer's stream with all judgments, across all revisions: the convergence trajectory of one mind against the model.
4.4 Judgments and authoring-frame provenance
Judgments are stored as ordinary assertions in arbiter streams, which means they replicate, branch, supersede, and tombstone exactly like any other testimony, and the system needs no second storage mechanism for them. Two conventions give them their power. Judgments reference their subjects by stable identity and revision, so a judgment is pinned to the exact version of the claim it evaluated. And judgments are spatially co-located: a judgment about an edge is keyed into the judgment space at the subject's coordinates, so the question of which judgments apply within this region of the building is itself a spatial range query. Symmetrically, every assertion may carry authoring-frame provenance: the frame identifier and revision ceiling its author was reading when the claim was made. This single field upgrades arbitration from detection to diagnosis, distinguishing a genuine mistake (inconsistent even with its own authoring frame) from staleness (consistent with a frame the world has moved past) from a perception error (consistent with the wrong frame, as when an engineer reads the wrong slice of the building). The error corpus this accumulates is permanent, spatially queryable, and the raw material of the convergence capabilities in Section 3.3.
5. Storage and Index Architecture Changes
5.1 Polarity in the endpoint index
The reverse endpoint index currently answers only the symmetric question of incidence. Directional queries require the index itself to know polarity; post-filtering after fetch is fake directionality that defeats the purpose on high-fan-out nodes. Three structural options were evaluated.
OptionMechanismAssessmentPolarity as a coordinate dimension (selected)Insert a polarity value into the index point between the endpoint coordinates and the edge-identifier dimensions. Directional lookup pins the polarity dimension; symmetric lookup ranges over it.Most faithful to the geometry-is-the-index principle: direction filtering becomes a narrower box, and bbox decomposition, interval scans, and sharding apply unchanged. Costs one dimension of the index budget and a layout migration of the reserved space.Dual reserved spaces (out-index and in-index)Tail-side records in one reserved space, head-side in another; neutral endpoints written to both or a third.Clean prefix semantics and no layout change to existing records, but forks the index machinery and makes symmetric incidence a union of two spaces.Polarity in the payloadStore polarity in the record payload and filter after fetching and decoding.Cheapest to ship and rejected as a destination: a backward traversal over a hub still fetches every outgoing edge to discard it. Acceptable only as the transitional behavior of Milestone 1.The selected design keeps endpoint space and coordinates as the leading prefix, so all existing incidence queries remain prefix scans; the polarity dimension follows the coordinates and precedes the edge-identifier dimensions, so direction is a range constraint and the unpinned case reproduces today's symmetric behavior exactly. Deletion tombstones the directional records symmetrically with how they were written. Because sealed blocks are immutable, the layout change is governed by a format version on the reserved space's registry entry: old and new layouts coexist, queries consult the version, and compaction performs the lazy rewrite.
5.2 Directional traversal
Traversal gains a direction policy (forward, backward, or both) and becomes arrival-aware: in forward mode, expansion from a node follows only edges in which that node sits in the tail set, and enqueues only the head set. Endpoints sharing the arrival side are co-causes, not destinations; the specification distinguishes expanding into them from merely reporting them. Traversal results surface wave-front level assignments, which under directional expansion are the topological strata of the dependency structure. A separate traversal mode implements B-connectivity, the conjunctive semantics unique to directed hypergraphs: a head node is reached only when all tails of some connecting edge have been reached. B-connectivity tracks per-edge satisfaction counts rather than a simple visited set and is the correct model for structural stability and for derived-value recomputation; it is a mode, not the default, because its cost profile differs from plain reachability.
5.3 The derivation bus
Every index record, locator entry, centroid key, and flow vector is a pure, deterministic function of the assertion that produced it. The refactor formalizes this as a derivation bus: the committed assertion stream feeds a fan-out stage that splits each edge into independent endpoint-pair tasks; a wide derivation stage performs coordinate packing and Hilbert encoding with no shared state; a routing stage groups finished records by Hilbert shard; and a sharded append stage writes into per-shard live tails of the derived spaces, each sealing independently. The unit of parallelism is the endpoint-pair, finer than the edge. The only ordering that matters is per-edge (an edge's deletion must not be processed before its insertion), obtained by hash-partitioning the work stream on edge identity; cross-edge order is irrelevant because every derived record carries its source revision and MVCC resolves visibility. Derived structures need no durability of their own: derivation is idempotent because the derived key is a deterministic function of the source, so crash recovery is re-derivation, consistent with the existing principle that sealed truth is rebuildable and scaffolding is disposable. Intent Checkpoints are the batch trigger, preserving the invariant that no derived structure ever reflects half of a logical operation. Per-session WALs add session-level parallelism above edge-level parallelism.
Each derived structure carries a watermark: the revision through which it is complete. A query at or below the watermark is exact through the index alone. A query above it merges index results with a direct scan of the unindexed delta, the recent assertions still resident in live tails, and is exact again; the seam between the two regimes is a single revision number. Callers may opt into index-only reads for bounded staleness. The watermark also provides principled backpressure: delta growth beyond a threshold widens the worker pool, throttles ingestion, or temporarily reverts a session to synchronous derivation. The bus is open-ended by design: the directional endpoint index, the edge locator, centroid keys, flow vectors, staleness markers, and future statistics are all subscribers, each with its own watermark, none on the write path. The cost inversion is the point: in this design a new derived structure costs background compute and a watermark, and the synchronous write path never grows.
5.4 Flow-vector geometry
With tails and heads structural, every directed edge yields a derived vector from tail centroid to head centroid. In a physically grounded space this vector is the physical direction of the relationship's flow, and it is computed in the derivation stage from data already in hand. Stored as derived coordinates (optionally as a quantized direction alongside the centroid key), it makes plausibility a geometry problem: gravity load paths point downward and span roughly one story, so an edge asserting that a fourth-floor beam bears on a second-floor column lands in a region of the edge space where no valid edge exists, and an arbiter finds it with a range scan over the whole building rather than by inspecting edges one at a time. This lane is promoted from research appendix to core design because it is on the critical path of the validation story: it is what makes continuous, index-resident arbitration affordable.
5.5 Frame resolution made index-resident
The efficient query of truth decomposes into four index-supported steps. Stream admission first: the session component of the HLC revision identifies the asserting stream within the key material the system already stores, so admitting or excluding streams is a filter over data co-resident with every record, and branch overlays already provide the machinery for reading a named subset of history. Spatial narrowing second: the query's region decomposes into Hilbert intervals exactly as today, unchanged by any of this. Supersession third: within the admitted streams, the existing MVCC machinery (latest admitted revision wins, tombstones and validity windows respected) resolves which assertions are current in the frame; supersession is deliberately modeled as the existing revision mechanics scoped to a frame rather than as a new mechanism. Judgment overlay fourth: because judgments are spatially co-located with their subjects, the consulted arbiters' judgments for the query region are fetched by the same interval scan pattern, then applied per the frame's policy (suppress, annotate, or select-contested). The performance contract is that a frame query costs a small constant number of interval scans more than a plain spatial query, and never a join proportional to history. Frames themselves are named, durable definitions, so applications reference them stably and arbiters can be defined as operating over one frame and emitting into another.
5.6 What does not change
The refactor is large in meaning and surprisingly conservative in substrate. Append-only writes, immutable sealed blocks, MVCC, Hilbert keying, sharding, branches, per-session WALs, HLC revisions, and Intent Checkpoints all survive unmodified in concept; most of them turn out to have been quietly designed for this model already. An append-only store was always a store that never unsays anything; per-session WALs were always parallel testimony; collision detection with deferred resolution was always an arbiter pattern one step from being named. The single global revision counter is the main conceptual casualty: global order is demoted from a write-time precondition to a read-time construction over HLC stamps.
6. Refactor Plan
The work is staged so that every milestone lands value independently, the system remains releasable between milestones, and the riskiest migrations are isolated. The interior-mutability and Send/Sync refactor already planned is a peer track; Milestone 4 depends on it and is its strongest motivating workload.
1. Milestone 1 — Directed model and validation. Endpoint polarity and edge directionality enter the model with versioned encoding; absent polarity decodes as undirected, which is the honest description of existing data. Validation and kind-catalog directionality policy land. The index remains symmetric and direction is post-filtered: slow but correct, and sufficient for adapters (including IFC import, whose relating and related sides map directly onto tail and head) to begin asserting direction structurally.
2. Milestone 2 — Polarity-dimension endpoint index. The reserved index space gains the polarity dimension under a registry format version; dual-layout reads, tombstone symmetry, and compaction-driven lazy rewrite land together. Directional incidence and degree queries become index-resident.
3. Milestone 3 — Directional traversal. Direction policy, arrival-aware expansion, wave-front level assignments, and per-kind acyclicity checks. B-connectivity ships as a distinct mode with its own tests and cost documentation.
4. Milestone 4 — Derivation bus. Fan-out, parallel derivation, shard routing, per-shard live tails, watermarks, delta-merge reads, and backpressure. The endpoint index and edge locator become the first subscribers; bulk import and steady-state ingestion converge on the same pipeline. Depends on the Send/Sync track.
5. Milestone 5 — Provenance and judgments. Authoring-frame provenance on assertions; judgment record conventions (subject identity plus revision pinning, spatial co-location); arbiter streams as ordinary streams. No new storage machinery, only conventions and key layouts, but this milestone is where the epistemology becomes operational.
6. Milestone 6 — Frame-resolution query API. Named durable frames; the three-axis query (streams, judgment overlay, as-of); supersession scoped to frames via existing MVCC mechanics; the performance contract of Section 5.5 enforced by tests.
7. Milestone 7 — Flow-vector lane and staleness closures. The flow-vector subscriber on the bus; quantized-direction keying for anomaly range scans; forward staleness closure and backward freshness checks built on directional traversal, completing the source-revision capability.
Compatibility throughout rests on three rules: sealed blocks are never rewritten in place; every encoding change is versioned with a defined decode for legacy records; and every derived structure is rebuildable from assertions alone, so a failed or interrupted migration of a derived space is recovered by re-derivation, never by surgery.
7. Costs, Risks, and Open Questions
Write amplification grows with the polarity dimension and with each derivation subscriber; the mitigation is that all growth is background, but background compute is still compute, and the watermark lag under import floods needs measurement, not assumption. The reserved index space migration is the riskiest single change in the plan and is why it is isolated as its own milestone with dual-layout reads. B-connectivity has a different memory and complexity profile from plain BFS and must not silently become the default. The delta-merge read path adds a class of tests the system has never needed: pipeline lag scenarios, crash-during-derivation idempotency, and tombstone-ordering races.
Three risks are epistemic rather than mechanical, and the document names them so they arrive by decision rather than surprise. First, convergence is only toward truth if frames are anchored: anchor streams (surveys, scans, as-built measurements) deserve explicit privileged status and their own validation, because a population converging confidently on a mis-registered scan is the failure mode of the whole theory. Second, replicas that must agree on a recomputed frame require arbiters that are deterministic and order-insensitive over the same assertion set; subjective arbiters escape this because their judgments replicate as assertions, but recomputed-everywhere arbiters need the confluence discipline stated in their contracts. Third, the error corpus is professionally sensitive: per-engineer trajectories power both anticipatory help and workplace surveillance, the append-only substrate forecloses quiet deletion, and access policy over judgment streams is an application-layer obligation this storage design makes urgent rather than optional.
Open questions carried forward: whether neutral endpoints in directed edges are permitted or every endpoint of a directed edge must take a side; the quantization granularity of flow-vector keying; whether frames may compose (a frame defined over another frame's output) in the first release or later; and how far the global revision counter is retained as a convenience ordering during the transition.
8. Principles
Four sentences govern the refactor and should be tested against every future design decision. Commit testimony synchronously, but only to its own stream. Derive geometry concurrently, behind watermarks, and never on the write path. Truth is not stored; it is the limit of a sequence of frame queries, and the database's job is to make the sequence converge fast. Synchronize at actuation boundaries, never at assertion boundaries.
InfiniteDB remains exactly what it set out to be: a storage mechanism. What has changed is the understanding of what is worth storing. Not the state of a model, which is one arbiter's opinion, but the full, parallel, spatially indexed testimony of everyone who ever worked on it, with direction in its relationships, geometry in its assertions, and judgment as data. The randomness of how people actually work is not a problem this design tolerates; it is the input this design was shaped for.
